# Decisions

Why the product is built the way it is. The main decisions come first, each with the alternatives considered; the rest follow, grouped by area. Each points at the code that carries it, where a `// Decision:` comment gives the local reasoning. Tests are described in [testing.md](testing.md), the system in [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## Main decisions

### 1. The model proposes, code decides

Context: a language model can invent places, misread opening hours, and return broken JSON, and travelers act on the plan.
Decision: Claude only picks a base per day and orders place ids from a shortlist, with a short reason per stop and a summary. The shortlist gives each base enough places for a whole trip there at the pace (every day's visits plus two, counting only places open on every trip date and one per spot, and a lunch and a dinner place a day plus one, `plan/candidates.ts`) and leaves out a place the traveler did not ask for that cannot be a day's only stop on any trip date (Osteria Francescana's dinner at a balanced pace), and the prompt says the visit limit is a maximum rather than a target, that every day needs a stop, that each id is used once, and that a missing meal beats a repeated place (`llm/prompt.ts`): with 12 visits and 5 meal places a base, the owner's failed plan of 2026-09-25 spent three days in Rome, repeated days 1 and 2 on its Sunday, and fell back once the tidy step had emptied that day. Under a budget, the shortlist also offers meal places one price level over it, marked "over budget, meals only" and after every meal place within it, as the rules-only planner may seat them (`isMealFallback` in `packages/planner/src/pools.ts`); the plan carries their `OVER_BUDGET` warning. Prompt v3 gives each base a meal supply line (how many meal places can take lunch and dinner each day, named on a scarce day, `llm/promptUser.ts`), says how to count a day's meal places from it and that a meal place is only a meal, placed where its meal happens, and names the base beside each must-include: with v2, 53% of AI days lacked a lunch or a dinner (28% of rules-only days), and at the lowest budget every day did. Live on the failure hunt's 56 request shapes and the prover's 12, days missing a meal fell to 37% (rules-only 24%) and lunches and dinners rose from 1.27 to 1.48 a day, with no fallback and every first answer valid after tidying. The call uses structured outputs (`output_config.format` with a JSON schema, `services/api/src/llm/anthropic.ts`), the answer is parsed with Zod (`llm/schema.ts`), ids outside the shortlist are errors (`plan/materialize.ts`), the scheduler assigns every time and the independent validator checks the result. Before the check, code tidies what the model cannot see because code assigns the times (the owner's call, 2026-09-24: "code tidies, AI chooses"): it drops a place on its closed day, a repeat or a second place at one spot, and ordinary visits over the pace's limit, and puts a day the scheduler cannot time in an order the rules-only planner's day walk finds for the same places (`plan/tidy.ts`, `packages/planner/src/orderDay.ts`). When a day still holds more than its opening hours allow, it drops an ordinary visit (the latest one the hours cannot hold, or else the latest one, which frees time for what follows it) and orders the day again until it times cleanly, never a must-include or a meal the day needs, and keeps only drops that lower the day's errors (on the 45 first answers of the live eval of 2026-09-25, 43 then passed the check, against 14 without this step). Then it puts each place dropped for the hours back into the day's final order where it fits after all (the Bargello, dropped at 14:50, fits as the first stop), moving nothing else, only where the day keeps its meals and its visit limit and the check finds nothing new, and at the position that gives the day the most meals and then moves the other stops least. A restaurant, or another place that serves meals, goes back only as a lunch or dinner the day lacks, never as a visit, and then also when the visit limit dropped it, since as a meal it adds no visit (on those answers 12 of the 59 such drops come back and so does one restaurant the visit limit dropped, on 3,000 random answers 547 and 54, and none of those plans stops passing). A day the repeat rules would empty keeps one of its places, taken instead from the day that had it first, a day they would leave with meal places only keeps one of its visits the same way when the day that had it first keeps another, and a place a day drops for its closed day, the visit limit, or the hours moves to another day at its base that holds it the same way, the day with the fewest stops first, and a restaurant the day's order times as a visit moves to where it is the lunch or dinner the day lacks (on 293 recorded answers, the 12 that tidied to an empty day all pass, 138 more stops are kept, and meals rise from 1.21 to 1.30 a day). Where one move is not enough, the day takes the nearest order of its own stops that gives its meal places more of their meals, a meal place over the budget that is still a visit moves to a day at its base that lacks the meal or is dropped (`over_budget_visit`), and of a meal place and a visit at one spot (the Mercato Centrale's food hall and market) the meal place stays when the trip then has more meals (on the 220 recorded v3 answers, missing meals fall from 274 to 245 and meal places timed as visits from 41 to 24, and no plan that passed fails). It never changes a base or adds a place the answer does not have, and it keeps a must-include the model put only on its closed day, so the check reports the closure instead of a plan passing without it. A tidied plan is labelled `ai_repaired` and the log line lists what was tidied. The evals still count a mistake the tidy step fixed as the model's (`packages/evals/src/measure.ts`), and "first-pass valid" means the first answer passed exactly as written. One repair turn gets the exact violations that remain, with each stop the tidy step removed and why, and the unused candidates open on any day left empty (`plan/repairNotes.ts`); every other outcome uses the rules-only plan (`plan/planTrip.ts`, `plan/outcome.ts`). Reasons and the summary are sanitized before they are shown.
Alternatives: let the model write the timetable (every time and travel claim would need checking and fixing); a forced tool call (a 400 on Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1, while structured outputs work on every current model, so the call stays portable); no repair (more fallbacks); several repairs (they eat the 24 s deadline).
Why: the worst the model can do is a weaker choice among valid places. An invalid plan cannot leave the function.
Revisit if: evals show the model orders days better than the scheduler's timing allows, a repair rarely succeeds, or tidying drops many places for lack of time (47 over the 45 first answers of 2026-09-25, after the put-back): the model is choosing more than its days hold.

### 2. Source notes only ever make hours stricter

Context: seasonal notes and free-text hours mix restrictions ("Open April-October only", "Third weekend of each month only") with extensions ("Summer hours extend to 19:30").
Decision: restrictions become date rules (season, weekdays, day of month) and are applied; extensions are logged as `note_not_applied` and ignored; a restriction wording no rule understands is logged as `note_unread` and listed first in the data notes (`packages/planner/src/normalize/seasonRules.ts`, `normalize/noteGrammar.ts`).
Alternatives: apply every note; ignore notes and trust the hours field.
Why: a wrong "open" sends a traveler to a closed door, while a wrong "closed" only hides one option.
Revisit if: the data gains structured seasonal hours.

### 3. Five bases with day trips, not a radius

Context: the 103 places cluster in five cities (Rome, Florence, Milan, Venice, Bologna) plus satellite towns such as Siena, Pienza, Modena, Parma, Lake Como, Burano and Padua.
Decision: a city with at least 5 places is a base, and every other place joins its nearest base within 120 km (`MIN_PLACES_FOR_BASE`, `DAY_TRIP_MAX_KM` in `packages/planner/src/config.ts`, `anchors.ts`). A day visits only places of its base (`OUTSIDE_ANCHOR`), a trip uses at most 2 bases, and a second base must earn its transfer time (`TRANSFER_COST` in `planPolicy.ts`).
Alternatives: a 35 km radius around each base (every day trip in the data would be orphaned); free routing between any places (days that cross regions).
Why: every place belongs to exactly one base, day trips stay plannable, and each day stays in one area.
Revisit if: the data gains many small towns far from the five bases.

### 4. A four-band travel model with intercity rail

Context: there is no routing API, only coordinates.
Decision: straight-line distance in four bands, rounded up to 5 minutes, with 10 minutes between stops: walk up to 1.5 km at 4.5 km/h; local up to 20 km at 10 minutes plus 25 km/h; regional up to 150 km at 30 minutes plus 70 km/h; intercity beyond that at 45 minutes plus 170 km/h (`TRAVEL` in `config.ts`, `travel.ts`). Rome to Florence is 2 h 10 min and Rome to Milan 3 h 35 min. Legs inside the Venice lagoon too long to walk read "by vaporetto", and a leg onto or off a boat-only island (Burano, San Giorgio Maggiore) is never a walk.
Alternatives: one 80 km/h band (Rome to Milan would take 6.5 hours); a routing API (cost, latency, and a network call inside a planner that must run offline).
Why: realistic enough to plan a day, deterministic, free, and the same function serves the scheduler, the validator and the browser.
Revisit if: a routing API becomes available; the validator would then compare against it.

### 5. The rules-only planner is a product path

Context: plans must work with no key, during model outages and rate limits, and offline in the installed app.
Decision: `planDeterministic` (`packages/planner/src/plan.ts`) builds a full trip from rules alone in about 10 ms, returns identical JSON for the same input (no wall clock, ties broken by id), and never returns an error-level plan. It serves plans with no key, with `?mode=deterministic`, as the API's fallback, and in the browser when the API cannot answer (`apps/web/lib/planRequest.ts`). The source line under the trip's dates says which path made the plan and why.
Alternatives: show an error when the AI path fails; a simple top-N fallback.
Why: the traveler always gets a checked plan, local development needs no key, and the evals have a baseline.
Revisit if: evals show the AI path adds nothing measurable over it.

### 6. An independent validator

Context: plans come from the scheduler, the model, the traveler's edits, share links and stored plans.
Decision: `validateItinerary` (`packages/planner/src/validate.ts`, `validate/*`) never calls the scheduler. It recomputes travel, transfers, the hours on each date, day and meal windows, visit caps and must-include placeability from the plan itself, and checks claimed travel minutes (`WRONG_TRAVEL`). There are 27 codes, 19 errors and 8 warnings, with one severity table (`violations.ts`). The property tests add a third, first-principles re-check (`test/properties/hardRules.ts`).
Alternatives: trust the scheduler's output; validate model output only.
Why: a scheduler bug is caught instead of repeated, and the same check runs in the API and the browser.
Revisit if: a routing API replaces the travel model.

### 7. Static files plus one function, and one planner everywhere

Context: 103 places, three-day plans, no accounts. The installed app must keep planning offline, and every AI plan is a paid model call. Saved trips came later and live in one key-value table (decision 12), not in a server that renders pages.
Decision: the web app is a Next.js static export served by CloudFront from a private S3 bucket (`apps/web/next.config.ts`). The API is one Hono app on one Lambda function behind an API Gateway HTTP API (`services/api/src/lambda.ts`); the same app runs on Node for local development (`local.ts`). The planner is one pure TypeScript package with no I/O (`packages/planner`), bundled into both the function and the browser.
Alternatives: a Next.js server in a container (a server to run and patch for pages that never change per request) or on Vercel (a second platform next to AWS, and the planner would still have to run in the browser for offline use); a backend in another language (the validator would be written twice, and the two could disagree); a browser-only app (the API key would have to reach the browser).
Why: nothing runs between requests, so there is no server to patch and an idle month costs little. The browser checks every plan and plans offline with exactly the rules the server uses.
Revisit if: the product gains accounts, or trips that must be listed or edited on the server (a database with queries, and perhaps server rendering, would then earn their place), or a plan must stream for longer than API Gateway's 30 s cap.

### 8. Layered spend limits on a public Claude endpoint

Context: anyone can call `POST /api/plan`, and Anthropic bills the model calls, so the AWS budget never sees them.
Decision: five layers, from the edge inward. The WAF allows 30 plan calls per IP per 5 minutes at the edge, on any spelling of the path (`infra/terraform/platform/waf.tf`). API Gateway allows the plan route 1 request a second with a burst of 6, and the function has 10 reserved instances (`infra/sam/template.yaml`). Inside each instance a token bucket allows 10 plans a minute per client (`services/api/src/lib/rateLimit.ts`), and the plan cache answers repeats from the tab, the instance or the table (decision 14). By default a plan makes at most three model calls (a first answer, one repair, one retry of a brief failure), each capped at 8,000 output tokens and all inside the 24 s deadline. Rules-only plans (no key, `?mode=deterministic`, the smoke tests) make no model call.
Alternatives: sign-in or an API key for travelers (friction for a planner anyone can open); the WAF rule alone (a flood from many addresses passes it); no reserved concurrency (a flood could also starve the other stacks in the shared account).
Why: each layer covers what the one before cannot. The WAF stops one client at the edge, the gateway and the reserved instances cap all clients together, the bucket covers calls that reach the function, and the cache makes repeats free.
Revisit if: real traffic reaches the gateway throttle (raise it together with the reserved instances), or the product gains accounts (limit per account instead of per address).

### 9. Terraform bootstrap, platform and SAM, applied by different hands

Context: the AWS account is shared with other production stacks, and CI deploys through GitHub OIDC. IAM cannot scope the HTTP API, CloudFront distributions, origin access control, headers policy or certificate by name, and tag conditions are only as strong as the right to tag.
Decision: `infra/terraform/bootstrap` (admin only) holds the CI roles, the permissions boundary, the artifacts bucket and the budget. `infra/sam` (Lambda, HTTP API, logs, alarms) and `infra/terraform/platform` (certificate, DNS, bucket, both distributions, WAF, origin secret) are created by an admin once and updated by CI. The deploy role cannot change its own roles or policies, cannot create a role without the boundary, and is denied the Anthropic key (`bootstrap/deploy-guardrails.tf`). The six ids AWS generated on the first deploy are committed in `infra/terraform/bootstrap/deployed-ids.auto.tfvars`, and every CI right on those resource types names exactly those ids (`arns.tf`, `pins.tf`): CI can update them but cannot create or delete them, and a `check` block warns while any id is empty. gitleaks allows id-shaped values in that one file only.
Alternatives: one root applied by CI (CI could widen its own rights); everything in SAM (the stack would carry the roles that deploy it); tag conditions or rights on every resource of a type instead of pinned ids.
Why: a compromised workflow cannot grant itself more, and each layer is changed by whoever should own it. Pinned ids are the only scope another stack's resources cannot match, and a change that would replace a pinned resource fails with AccessDenied before anything is deleted.
Revisit if: the project moves to an AWS account of its own, or a pinned resource must be replaced (an admin applies, then re-pins).

### 10. Same-origin /api for the browser, plus a public API host

Context: the web app needs the API, and other clients (the post-deploy smoke test, curl and scripts, future tool clients) want plain paths. The evals run the production pipeline in-process, so they need no network and no WAF allowance.
Decision: the site distribution serves the bucket at `/*` and the HTTP API at `/api/*`, so the browser calls its own origin: no CORS in production, and `connect-src` is this origin only (the map tiles are served from the site too). A second distribution at `api.italy-planner.brdjx.com` has origin path `/api`, the same WAF, headers policy and origin secret, and sends no CORS headers (`infra/terraform/platform/cloudfront.tf`, `cloudfront-api.tf`).
Alternatives: the browser calls the API host (preflights on every plan, a second host in the CSP); site paths only for scripts.
Why: one WAF path covers every browser call, and the public host opens no way around the WAF or the origin check.
Revisit if: a browser client on another origin needs the API (it would need CORS).

### 11. Tests start from failure vectors

Context: the product fails badly in a few specific ways: an invalid plan, a hung model call, a leaked secret, runaway cost, a bad deploy.
Decision: twelve failure vectors, F1 to F12, each with a guard in code and tests named after the failure they prevent ([testing.md](testing.md)): fixed-seed property tests, a validator mutation suite with a corruption for every error code, a scripted model client for every API failure, E2E on six device profiles plus the installed app, `terraform test` with mocked providers, and a post-deploy smoke test. Coverage floors per area sit at the measured figures, so they only move up.
Alternatives: a coverage target alone; example tests only.
Why: each test proves a guard holds, and deleting a check fails a named test.
Revisit if: a failure reaches production that no vector covers; add the vector, then the test.

### 12. Saved trips: one DynamoDB table and a short link, opened as saved

Context: Copy link put the trip's ids in the URL (`?p=`), and the page that opened it timed the ids again with rule why lines. The AI's why lines, the summary and how the trip was planned were lost, and nothing in such a link could be trusted as the AI's words, since anyone can write one. The owner decided (2026-09-25): "Saved trip, short link".
Decision: Copy link saves a snapshot of the trip as the traveler sees it and copies `?t=<id>`, 10 base62 characters from a random source (`apps/web/components/ShareButton.tsx`, `lib/copyLink.ts`). `POST /api/trips` takes ids only, with a strict schema, and rebuilds the trip itself: the planner times it and gives rule why lines, and the AI's why lines and summary come back only from the API's own records, either the AI plan's record, kept 90 days under the `planId` `/api/plan` returns, or the saved trip it was opened from. Each stored line passes the text checks and the planner's claim check again for the stop as timed, the summary is cleaned again for the trip's places, and a trip with a validator error is refused (422) (`services/api/src/routes/trips.ts`, `trips/rebuild.ts`). The snapshot is kept a year and `GET /api/trips/:id` returns it exactly as stored. The page shows it as saved, with how it was planned, or times it again from its ids when its place data differs from the page's (the planner's `dataVersion`, also in `/api/meta`) (`apps/web/lib/savedTrip.ts`). Storage is one DynamoDB table, on demand, with time to live, point-in-time recovery, `Retain` on delete and deletion protection, which the function may only `GetItem` and `PutItem` (`infra/sam/template.yaml`). When saving fails, or the plan has stops that break a rule (the server would refuse it), Copy link copies the `?p=` link and says why, and `?p=` links open as before.
Alternatives: a longer link that carries the why lines and summary (the words would come from the link, so anyone could put text on a trip under the AI's name, and links would grow past what chat apps show); S3 objects (fine for writes once and reads by key, but expiry by lifecycle rules per prefix rather than per item, and a bucket, its policy and its versioning to own for records of a few KB); Postgres (a database server or Aurora to run, patch and pay for when idle, and VPC networking for the function, for a lookup by key).
Why: the content of a saved trip comes from our store, not the URL, so a link cannot forge it. A table with one key and a time to live is the smallest store for "write once, read by id, expire", costs nothing when idle, and IAM can hold the function to two actions on one table. A store failure never costs a plan: the plan goes out without a `planId`, and Copy link falls back to `?p=`.
Revisit if: trips need to be listed, edited in place or tied to accounts (queries and a user key), links must never expire, or the page keeps its last plan longer than 90 days (the plan record's lifetime, `KEEP_SECONDS` in `services/api/src/trips/store.ts`, matches `LAST_PLAN_MAX_AGE_DAYS` so every plan the page can show saves with its AI why lines; a test holds the two together).

### 13. The traveler's notes stay out of saved trips

Context: the notes ("Anything else?") are private, and requests are stored without them. The AI can still repeat them: its summary is asked to mention them, and a why line can say "a calm stop that suits your recent surgery". Those passed every text check, so a saved trip would have shown them to anyone with the link for a year. The owner's delegate decided (2026-09-25) to leave that text out, and after review chose the simplest guarantee: none of the AI's text.
Decision: when a plan was made with notes, its plan record keeps none of the AI's text, no summary and no why line; it still says the AI planned it, with the model and prompt version. A saved trip of it has no summary and the rule's why line on every stop. Such plans never go into the shared plan cache in the table either (decision 14). The rule is the planner's (`hasNotes`, `privateAiText` in `packages/planner/src/privateText.ts`); the API applies it when it writes the record (`services/api/src/trips/records.ts`), and when Copy link saves such a plan that had AI text, the page says so under the header and in the status line: "Link copied. To keep your notes private, the shared trip leaves out the AI's summary and why lines." The traveler's own view keeps every word.
Alternatives: leaving out only the summary and the why lines that address the traveler (you, your and the like), the first version of this rule (it kept more of the AI's words, but an echo need not say "you", as in "a calm stop after knee surgery"); matching the notes' words (the model paraphrases); telling the traveler and storing it anyway; not saving plans made with notes at all (the trip itself holds no notes and is worth sharing).
Why: it is the one rule that holds whatever the model writes, and it is easy to say to the traveler and to check: the record of a plan with notes has no why lines and no summary. Leaving the text out when the record is written means the table never holds it, and a trip saved again from a saved trip cannot bring it back. The cost is the AI's why lines on shared trips of plans with notes, which the rule's lines replace.
Revisit if: travelers want to share what the AI wrote for their notes on purpose (a choice when copying), or a check that tells an echo of the notes from a line about the place becomes reliable.

### 14. An AI plan cache in three layers, keyed on the options

Context: the owner asked (2026-09-25) to cache plans by the selected options so the same options do not run AI inference again. The API had an in-memory cache per Lambda instance, keyed on the request with its lists in the order sent, so a second instance, or the same chips in another order, paid for the model again.
Decision: a plan is looked up in the tab (`apps/web/lib/planMemo.ts`, at most 20 plans, the tab's memory), in the instance (an LRU of 100), then in the table (item `cache#<key>`, 7 days, read with a 150 ms limit), and only then asked of the model. The key is the planner's `planRequestKey`: interests, must-includes and skips sorted, the bases in their order (the first is the planner's first choice), the notes as typed. The API adds the prompt version, the model, the deployed commit and the place data's fingerprint and hashes it (`services/api/src/lib/cache.ts`, `plan/planCache.ts`). Only AI plans are cached; the table gets a plan only with its `planId` and never one made with notes; a table failure is a miss. The page shows a plan from its memory with the same source line and "Your plan is ready", and no "shown from this session" note: it is the very plan the API returned for these options, the API would answer with it again, and a note would suggest it differs or is stale. Edits never write into any layer, so planning the same options again shows the plan as it came. Every layer answers with the request as sent now, so the options show in the order just chosen; a rule why line that names two interests keeps the order of the request it was made for, since re-deriving it needs each stop's timed day.
Alternatives: sessionStorage for the tab (it outlives a reload, when the last plan already comes back, and would keep a plan across a deploy that changed the data); a longer table lifetime (hours and seasons drift; 7 days also keeps every cached plan well inside the 90 days of the plan record its `planId` points at); caching rules-only answers (usually a fallback that asking again may improve); a note on plans from memory.
Why: most repeats come from the same traveler going back and forth between options, which the tab answers with no request at all; the table makes a plan made on one instance free on the other nine. Sorting only the lists that are sets means a hit is always a plan that meets every rule for the request.
Revisit if: the model's answers should vary between asks (a "plan again" that skips the cache), plans must reflect data changes within a week without a data deploy, or the table's cache items grow past what on-demand pricing makes negligible.

### 15. Re-plan one day of a trip

Context: the owner asked (2026-09-25) to change the city of any day of a planned trip ("day 2 change city, day 3 change city") and have that day filled again, by the AI once a plan exists, while never repeating a place the trip already has and planning only from the data. The planner could only plan whole trips, and the trip route plans all three days at once.
Decision: `POST /api/plan/day` plans one day of the trip the page has, at a base the traveler picked, and never changes another day. The body is ids only (the request, each day's base and place ids, the day, the base, and places to leave out of a new version of the day), checked against the data: a place on two days, in another day's base, or an empty day other than the one being planned is a 400. The cities a day may take come from the planner (`dayBaseOptions` in `packages/planner/src/dayBases.ts`, which the page lists and the route enforces with a 422 and the same reason): at most two bases in the trip and one change of base, as the whole-trip planner's arrangements have (`planAnchors.ts`), so Rome, Florence, Rome is refused with the day to move first (Rome, Rome, Florence, then Rome, Florence, Florence), a day holding a must-include keeps its city, and the rules-only day there (`planDay` in `packages/planner/src/planDay.ts`: the whole-trip day walk, must-include repair and meal fill, on this day only, with the other days' places counted as used) must not be empty and must leave the trip with no error it did not already have, the next day included, since its transfer changes. The AI path is the trip path narrowed to a day: a shortlist of every place of the base allowed on that date that no other day has, not at the same spot as one, and not excluded or left out (`plan/dayShortlist.ts`); a short system prompt of its own that lists the places already used (`llm/dayPrompt.ts`, `day-v1`); a one-day answer schema; the trip's tidy step on the trip with this day in it, after dropping any repeat of another day (`plan/dayTidy.ts`); an id outside the shortlist is an error; scheduleTrip and the validator on the whole trip; one repair turn; and the rules-only day for every other outcome (`plan/replanDay.ts`). The answer is the day alone, timed in its trip, with its source and meta; the page applies it with `withReplannedDay`, which times the next day again. AI days are cached like plans (memory, then the table, never with notes), keyed on the day prompt, model, commit, data, request, the other days, the day, the base and the places left out (`plan/dayCache.ts`).
Alternatives: re-plan the whole trip with the new base pinned (every other day could change, which is not what the traveler asked for); move the next day too when it no longer fits (it changes a day the traveler did not touch); allow a day away and back whenever the validator passes it (the first build did: the validator's only base rule is two bases, but its must-include check assumes one change of base, `validate/mustIncludeFit.ts`, and a review sweep of 17,280 such moves allowed 10.7%, Milan, Florence, Milan among them at 2 h 15 min each way for one day); tell the model the used places but offer the whole base (a repeat would then need the tidy step every time, and the model would plan from places it cannot have).
Why: the model can only choose among places the day may really take, code proves the day against the whole trip, and the other days are the traveler's. Live on 24 re-plans with Sonnet 5 at production settings (2026-09-25): every day valid, no repeat of another day and no id outside the shortlist in any answer, no repair turn and no fallback, 3.3 to 5.5 s a day, about 2,800 input and 280 output tokens, $0.10 for 12.
Revisit if: travelers often want the day after a move to follow it (a two-day change in one request), the day's order is reordered by the tidy step on most answers (11 of 12 in the last run) and a prompt that states the times does measurably better, or saved trips should keep a re-planned day's AI why lines (they carry the rule's today, since the plan record holds the plan as first made).

## All decisions by area

### Data

- **Hours carry their source.** Derived and open-access windows are stored as hours, with `hoursConfidence` (`listed`, `derived`, `open_access`, `unknown`) driving the chips. Precedence: listed hours, free text ("Evenings" 18:00 to 24:00, "Morning only" 07:00 to 13:00), a time of day in the name, open access, unknown (`normalize/hours.ts`, `dataPolicy.ts`).
- **Name hints fill empty hours only.** "by Night" 20:00 to 24:00, "at Dawn" 06:00 to 10:00, "Early Morning" 06:00 to 11:00, "Aperitivo" 17:30 to 21:00; listed hours always win (`NAME_TIME_HINTS`).
- **Public spaces with no hours are open 07:00 to 23:00**, but only when the raw type is a public space; a ticketed church with no hours stays unknown.
- **Unknown hours still plan**, inside the day window, ranked lower, with an `HOURS_UNKNOWN` warning.
- **Third weekend means a Saturday or Sunday on the 15th to the 21st**, and "last" counts back from the month's real end, so the rule is exactly as strict as the description.
- **A close before the open crosses midnight only when unambiguous** (closes by 06:00, spans at most 18 hours, and says "am", has a leading zero, or opens at 13:00 or later). "9-5" is not read as 20 hours open.
- **Wording that says a place is shut excludes the record** with reason `closed`; a closed part of a place is only a note.
- **Meals come from the hours.** A restaurant keeps lunch or dinner only if some open day can seat that meal, so five restaurants serve one meal. Eight non-restaurants are meal places through a reviewed allowlist with a reason each (`EXTRA_MEAL_PLACES` in `dataPolicy.ts`).
- **Visit lengths**: a missing length takes the type default, one outside the type's bounds is clamped, and one longer than the longest open range is cut to fit it.
- **Coordinates far from the city are repaired**: more than 40 km from the median of the city's other places moves the point to the median of its neighborhood, else its city (Brera market lands within 0.1 km of the Pinacoteca), shown as an approximate location.
- **Places and excluded records are separate lists**, so no consumer can plan an excluded record by mistake; places plus excluded always equals the records read.
- **Ids are resolved first** and kept when safe (`[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}`); they appear in URLs, prompts and map keys.
- **Duplicates merge by folded name and city.** Identical coordinates with different names (Trevi Fountain by day and by night) are kept and linked, and a trip holds at most one of them. Reviewed same-experience pairs are linked the same way.
- **Prices are levels 1 to 4**; "free" counts as 1, and a tag that disagrees with the price is logged, not applied.
- **Places rated below 3.5 are never suggested**, but a traveler can still ask for them.
- **Lookup tables ignore inherited keys** (`Object.hasOwn`), found by fuzzing: a price of `__proto__` once returned `Object.prototype`.
- **Text over 300 characters is never pattern-matched**, so hostile megabyte strings normalize in milliseconds.
- **Dates use `Date.UTC` only**, and the planner's `tsconfig.json` has no Node types, so a local-time call or a Node import fails a guard.
- **The source file is never edited.** Fixes live in code and reviewed tables, and a checksum test fails on any edit to `data/italy.json`.

### Planner

- **Roles are inferred from the order.** The model, share links and edits carry ids only; a meal place becomes the next meal it can seat if that meal starts within 60 minutes (`inferRole`, `schedule.ts`).
- **One timing function** (`timeStep`) serves the planner and `scheduleDay`, so re-timing a plan from its ids gives identical stops and a share link reproduces the plan.
- **Greedy days with one look-ahead.** Each pick keeps the meal on offer reachable, using latest start times (`dayLimits.ts`, `dayPicks.ts`). Two picks look at the other days: a meal goes to the meal place with the fewest meals left elsewhere, and a morning sight no later day can hold beats a nearly as good pick.
- **Days take turns by clock**, earliest clock first, so day 3 no longer gets the leftovers (`tripWalk.ts`).
- **Bases are chosen by building whole trips** for the top 3 bases (plus any base holding a must-include), alone and in pairs, ranked by must-includes kept, then score minus transfer cost; a mistimed must-include costs 20 points (`tripBuilder.ts`, `planPolicy.ts`).
- **Chosen bases are kept** as long as each day can hold a place there, except where the walk gives one day the only place another day could use: 14 of 19,962 such requests in a measurement, bounded at 1 in 1000 by a property test. A day away from a chosen base always says so with `ANCHOR_NOT_CHOSEN`.
- **Passes after the walk never break a hard rule**: the must-include repair (removing as few ordinary stops as it can) and the meal fill (`mustRepair.ts`, `mealFill.ts`); an empty day may take one public space (`tripWalk.ts`). Every rule was kept or removed by measuring the planner without it on three seeds; the route pass, meal sharing, and the other rescues did not clear the bar. The numbers are in [planner.md](planner.md#the-rules-in-the-order-they-run-and-what-each-buys).
- **Meal places are for meals**, and restaurants are never visits unless the traveler asked for them.
- **Outings** (a non-meal visit of 240 minutes or more) start by 12:00 unless the traveler asked for them, and cover a meal they span for 60 minutes; a day trip pays for at most 10 km of distance in the score.
- **Taste rules are planner preferences, not validator errors**: parks end by sunset, aperitivo after 17:00, gelato and wine bars after lunch, museums avoid 25 December and 1 January. They never keep out a must-include.
- **One day window, meals inside it.** Every stop fits the pace window, and the trip back to the base is a hard rule; after a final dinner it may end 30 minutes past the day end (`DINNER_RETURN_GRACE_MIN`).
- **Scores are rounded to 6 decimals and ties break by id** with plain comparison, never `localeCompare`, so the browser and the Lambda agree.
- **Headline sights lead when there are no interests** (an iconic bonus of 0.75); an interest match is worth up to 3.
- **The owner's flagship places get a small bonus** (the owner's call, 2026-09-25: extra emphasis for the Vatican). A reviewed list in `dataPolicy.ts` (`FLAGSHIP_PLACES`, the Vatican Museums) adds 0.06 to the score as its own part, never to a must-include. It is the largest bonus under the bar: in the mixed sweep, Rome trips where the Vatican Museums are open, within budget, and not asked for include them 79.6% of the time, not 75.5%. From 0.08 plain Rome trips, which already had them, reshuffle, and packed ones starting on a Sunday or a Monday miss a dinner ([planner.md](planner.md#the-rules-in-the-order-they-run-and-what-each-buys)).
- **A meal one price level over the budget** is used only when nothing within budget can take that meal, with an `OVER_BUDGET` warning. The AI shortlist offers the same places (`isMealFallback`), marked and after every meal place within budget, and the prompt asks for one only for a meal nothing within budget can take; the `budget-traveler` eval case allows the warning on a lunch or dinner and still forbids it on a visit (`allowOnMealStops`).
- **Swaps only offer what keeps the day valid**, checked by the scheduler and the validator (`alternatives.ts`).
- **One day planned again never touches another** (decision 15). `planDay` walks one day with the other days' places counted as used, and the must-include repair and meal fill run on that day only (their `only` argument). A must-include already on another day stays there; the day takes its base's must-includes the trip does not have yet. A trip changes base once at most, as the planner's arrangements do, so a day never goes away and comes back (Rome, Florence, Rome), and a move must leave the next day fitting after its new transfer; over 60 one-base trips, day 3 could move in all 60, day 1 in 33 and day 2 in none, and after day 3 moved, day 2 could follow in 59, so the refusal names the neighbouring day to move first when that works.
- **Warnings come from the validator only**, for new plans and edits alike.
- **Rule reasons' date sentences are the planner's own hours answer** for that stop and date ("It cannot be visited on Sunday, the trip's last day.", "Starts as it opens."), stated as facts, never as causes, and left out when the listing says more than the planner reads, when its note leaves the dates to the traveler (the risotto festival's "check exact festival dates"), and for every place on 25 December and 1 January, which have no holiday hours in the data (`reasons.ts`).
- **An AI reason is kept only while it holds for the stop as timed**: a meal, a part of the day, the sun, or a place in the day or trip that the stop does not bear out drops it for the rule reason (`packages/planner/src/reasonClaims.ts`). The API checks it on a new plan and logs `wrong_meal`, `wrong_time_of_day` or `wrong_position`; the planner checks it again whenever an edit on the page times the stop anew, so "to start the day" never follows a stop to third place. A meal word on a visit holds only somewhere to eat (a meal place, a cafe or restaurant, a place tagged food, or an outing under way through the meal), in that meal's window, on a day with no other stop for it, and the evening starts at sunset when the sun sets before 18:00.
- **Validator details**: the first stop is reached from the base; `SEASONAL_CLOSED` (another date helps) differs from `CLOSED_AT_TIME` (another time helps); a third meal counts as a visit; times are whole minutes up to 06:00 the next day; details are cut to 500 characters and never echo unknown ids.
- **Must-include placeability**: `MUST_INCLUDE_MISSING` is an error only when the plan clearly had room; otherwise `MUST_INCLUDE_UNPLACEABLE` explains why (`validate/mustInclude.ts`).
- **`TRIP_DAYS` is 3** and the suite passes at 3 and 4. A trip must end by 2100-12-31, so request and response schemas agree.

### API and AI

- **Length limits live in Zod only.** Structured outputs ignore them, and a long reason should cost that reason, not the whole answer.
- **A per-model settings map** (`llm/models.ts`): Sonnet 5 gets `effort` (default low) and no temperature, Haiku 4.5 gets temperature 0.2 and no effort; unknown ids get neither. `max_tokens` 8000.
- **The shortlist is enforced and borrows the fallback's bases**: up to 4 bases, each with enough visits and meal places for a whole trip there at the pace, meal places one price level over a budget marked for meals only; a base the rules-only plan needs is offered too (`plan/candidates.ts`). The largest request, four bases at the packed pace, stays under 25,000 characters, about 12,800 tokens, before the traveler's notes (at most 500 characters, 2,500 once escaped) (`test/unit/prompt.test.ts`).
- **Timing**: 24 s deadline from arrival, each call at most 15 s and at most what is left after a 1.5 s reserve, no first call under 2 s and no repair under 4 s, so a repair after a first answer at 15 s still gets 7.5 s. At 12 s a call, 1 of 166 live first calls timed out and fell back with 10.5 s unused; with 15 s, two first answers of 13.8 and 14.4 s became plans. Function timeout 29 s, API Gateway cap 30 s.
- **No SDK retries; one deadline-aware retry** for a dropped connection, a 5xx, an overload, or a 429 asking to wait at most 1 s.
- **A tidied plan is never "ai".** Only an answer that passes the check exactly as the model wrote it is labelled `ai`; one the tidy step changed is `ai_repaired` ("fixed after a check"; About this data's "How a plan is made" names both ways a draft is fixed), and the log's `tidied` field lists each change with its rule, day and place, and for a move to another day (`moved_day`) the rule that took it off its day as `cause` (`plan/planTrip.ts`, `plan/tidy.ts`, `apps/web/lib/sourceText.ts`).
- **Tidying keeps the model's order when the scheduler can time it**, and otherwise takes the walk (of two: the planner's own picks, or every place as soon as it can start) whose order times with the fewest errors, only when that is fewer than the model's. A day holding an id not offered or a place of another base keeps its order and length: the check sends it back anyway.
- **Tidying never takes a must-include out of the answer.** It is never trimmed or dropped for sharing a spot, and on its closed day it is dropped only when the answer also has it on a day it is open. Dropped from its only day, a plan could pass without it: when that day is the trip's only one at its base, the validator only warns (`MUST_INCLUDE_UNPLACEABLE`), though another order of bases would fit it. Kept, the check names the closure and its date, and the repair turn or the rules-only planner places it.
- **Every failure has a fallback reason** (`refusal`, `max_tokens`, `rate_limited`, `timeout`, `llm_error`, `schema_invalid`, `invalid_after_repair`, `no_key`, `disabled`, `requested`), shown in the UI and the log.
- **AI plans carry exactly the validator's warnings**, so the browser shows the same list.
- **Only AI plans are cached** (decision 14): in the tab (20), in each instance (100, as copies) and in the table for 7 days (with a `planId`, never with notes), keyed by prompt version, model, deployed commit, place data and the planner's `planRequestKey`; a fallback is never pinned. A hit answers with the request as sent.
- **Reasons and summary are grounded**: dropped for another place's name, a time, duration or price, links, contact or payment details, injection wording in six languages, prompt echoes, or a capitalized word the dataset never uses.
- **The origin check fails closed**, re-reads SSM at most every 10 s on a mismatch, and accepts the previous secret for 15 minutes after a rotation is seen.
- **The key is re-read every 15 minutes** and after a 401 or 403 (at most once a minute), so a rotated key reaches warm instances without a redeploy.
- **Rate limit key**: `CloudFront-Viewer-Address`, then the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry; IPv6 by /64.
- **JSON only** (415 otherwise), and the 16 KB cap counts bytes read, not the header.
- **Error bodies never reflect input**; a 500 carries a fixed message and the request id. No feasible plan is 422; a rules-only plan failing its guard is 503.
- **Read routes are cacheable** (strong ETag, `max-age=300, s-maxage=3600`), and so is a saved trip (`public, max-age=300, immutable`: it is written once and never changed); everything else is `no-store`.
- **AI plans are kept 90 days under a `planId`** (as long as the page keeps its last plan), their request without notes, ids, AI why lines and summary (without the text decision 13 leaves out), so a trip saved later carries them from the store. A cached plan lives 7 days, never longer than its record. The write has 1 s; a failure is logged, counted (`TripStoreFailures`) and never fails the plan. Rules-only plans get no `planId`.
- **A day re-plan is a plan, for limits and caching** (decision 15): the same origin check, JSON only and 16 KB cap, one per-client bucket shared with `POST /api/plan` (both spend model calls), its own gateway route (`POST /api/plan/day`, 1 a second, burst 3, so both model routes together stay under the 10 reserved instances), and the day cache beside the plan cache. The answer never includes another day, and a day made for notes stays out of the table.
- **Saving a trip is limited per client** (20 a minute per instance, its own bucket), and at the gateway on a route of its own (`POST /api/trips`, 2 a second, burst 10), because every saved trip is kept a year and a flood must not take the read routes' share. Opening a trip stays with the read routes.
- **A record is free to write once its time is up**: the conditional put accepts a missing key or an expired record, which `get` already treats as gone, so a cache key asked for again while DynamoDB is still deleting the old item can be cached again.
- **Metrics ride on the request log line** (Embedded Metric Format), so handled 500s and silent fallbacks can alarm without new IAM rights.
- **Config fails fast**: deadline at most 26 s, 2 or 3 attempts, `NODE_ENV=production` inside Lambda, fixture mode refused in production, the origin check on whenever its parameter is set.
- **CORS only outside production**, and the local server binds to 127.0.0.1.

### Web and PWA

- **Every API body is parsed with Zod**: strict for places and itineraries, loose for envelopes; schema errors carry field paths, never values.
- **The form works from the places alone**; `/api/meta` only refines labels.
- **The browser plans for anything except a refused request.** Network errors, timeouts, 5xx, 429 and unreadable replies get a local plan labelled with the real cause; a refused request (400, 413, 415, 422) shows the error and names the fields the API listed.
- **The page validates the API's plan** before showing it, and replaces a plan with errors by a local one.
- **Edits are kept and flagged, not blocked**, with up to 10 undo steps.
- **Change city is one edit, and the page decides what goes in** (decision 15). The city on each day's line opens a sheet with the planner's own verdict for every base (`dayBaseOptions`): the day's city first with New ideas for this day, a city the day cannot take shown in place with its reason, never hidden. Choosing sends `POST /api/plan/day`; that day's board becomes a skeleton while the rest of the trip stays in use, and one day plans at a time. The answer goes through the itinerary reducer, which takes the API's day only if it still fits the trip on screen (the day and city asked for, known places, none on another day, no new validator error) and otherwise plans the day here with `checkDayBase`, labelled as a rules plan with the cause; `withReplannedDay` then times the whole trip and the validator checks it, and one Undo takes it all back (`apps/web/lib/dayCity.ts`, `itineraryReducer.ts`, `useDayCity.ts`). A line under the day says how it was planned, in the source line's words with "again", ends ", edited" once the traveler changes a stop on that day, and is kept with the last plan. A day planned again from the sheet shows no "Not one of your bases" note (`shownWarnings`): the traveler picked its city, and the note's next step, Edit the trip to change your bases, would undo that; a saved link does not record which day moved, so it shows the note again. Alternatives: add the city to the request's bases instead (a saved trip would no longer match its plan record, `samePlanRequest`, so every day would lose its AI why lines, and the next Plan my trip would treat the city as chosen for every day); block edits on the other days while a day plans (the traveler asked for the rest to stay usable; an answer that no longer fits is planned again here instead, and blamed on the server only when the trip did not change); a whole-trip source for a day the rules planned (the trip's line cannot say which day).
- **New ideas leave out the day's own places**, not only the other days'. The rules are deterministic and AI days are cached by their input, so the same trip at the same city would give the same day back. Only the places on screen are left out, not every earlier version, so a small city does not run out after a few presses; a must-include is never left out.
- **A saved trip shows the rules' why lines on a day planned again**, and Copy link says which day ("Day 2 was planned again, so the saved trip shows the rules' why lines for it."). The server takes AI text only from the AI plan on record, which holds the plan as first made; keeping the page's copy would let a client write text into a saved trip. The saved trip still says it was planned with AI and edited.
- **Copy link saves the trip first** and copies its `?t=` link (decision 12); when saving fails it copies the `?p=` link and a line under the header says so. A plan with flagged stops is not sent at all (the server would refuse it): the `?p=` link is copied and the line says the trip was not saved because it breaks a rule, naming "a stop" or "stops" by how many are flagged. For a plan made with notes that had AI text, the line says the shared trip leaves out the AI's summary and why lines (decision 13). The clipboard write starts inside the press with the link as a promise (`ClipboardItem`), because Safari forgets the press at the first await; `writeText` after the save, then a field to copy by hand, cover other browsers. While saving, the pill keeps its shape and words and only its link mark fades.
- **A saved trip opens as saved** when the page has the same place data, and is timed again from its ids otherwise, with a note. A missing trip or a failed load leaves a note over the form; a failed load keeps `?t=` so a reload tries again, and a load that had already failed when the places arrived (the API was down, then Try again) is made once more; until then the note over the form says the saved trip will open once the connection is back. When the traveler plans a trip of their own while a `?t=` or `?p=` link is still on its way, the newer request wins: the link is given up and its parameter removed, so it never opens over their plan.
- **The AI summary follows the trip's places**: the page shows it without the sentences that name a place the trip no longer has, with the planner's `summaryForPlaces`, which the server runs on the same summary when it saves the trip, so sender and recipient read the same summary. Undo brings a sentence back.
- **A plan for options this tab already planned shows at once** from the tab's memory, with no request and nothing new in its source line, and with the request as sent now, as the API answers a hit (decision 14).
- **Share links carry ids only and never notes**, capped at 8 KB, and repair instead of failing: unknown or excluded ids are dropped, flagged stops are left out until the validator is clean, and every outcome shows a note.
- **The last plan is stored whole and read as hostile input**: discarded when over 100,000 characters, invalid, older than 90 days, past, or naming unknown places. It keeps whether the traveler had edited it, so after a reload the source line still says "edited", and a saved trip timed again with newer place data says so in the note over the plan.
- **The source line never claims more than the page knows**, including "Shared plan, rebuilt from its places" for links and "Saved trip" for saved links, where who planned it comes from the server's record ("Saved trip, planned with AI, saved 25 Sep"); without an AI plan on record it says "Saved trip" and the day, and claims neither the AI nor the rules.
- **The source line is one line of fact, not a disclosure** (the owner's decision). It says how the plan was made and, for a rules plan that was a fallback, why in a few words from the fallback reason or the browser's cause ("Planned without AI: the AI planner timed out", "Planned on this device, offline"), with ", edited" and, when a rule breaks, the count to fix; the drawn check stays, gold for the AI, ink for the rules. The general explanation (what every plan is checked against, both ways a draft is fixed, the reason dots, shared and saved links) is in About this data's "How a plan is made", and what one opened link left out or timed again is in the note over the plan (`apps/web/lib/sourceText.ts`, `components/SourceBadge.tsx`). Alternative: the tap-to-open details under the line, which put the plan's provenance a tap away and repeated the same general sentences on every plan.
- **The map is a visual aid** and the timetable its text equivalent; a failed map chunk, a missing worker or no WebGL shows a notice.
- **A self-hosted vector map, not Google Maps** (the owner's choice). MapLibre GL draws a Protomaps extract of only the areas the trip uses (about 140 MB, three zoom bands: `scripts/map-tiles/`), styled in the page's palette with a real dark mode. The file is uploaded once to the site bucket under `tiles/` and deploys never delete it; the page makes no third-party request, needs no key and tracks no one. MapLibre's worker and glyphs are precached so an offline plan still draws its route and stops; the tiles are not. Changing day glides the camera, or jumps under reduced motion.
- **The service worker is hand-written** (`apps/web/public/sw.js`) and tests run that exact file in a sandbox. `POST /api/plan` and all other API calls except `GET /api/meta` and `/api/places` bypass it.
- **Cache names come from a content hash**, and the shell's hash is checked at install.
- **Updates wait for a tap** ("A new version is available"); first installs are silent.
- **Safe areas and `100dvh`** live in CSS custom properties; targets are at least 44 px and inputs 16 px.
- **Zod runs jitless**, so the CSP needs no `unsafe-eval`.
- **One column on every screen.** Before a plan the page is the date, the pace, More options and Plan my trip; once a plan is on screen or on its way the form folds into a one-line trip summary with Edit trip, on phones and desktops alike. This replaces the build plan's two-pane desktop sketch: the plan gets the full width and the form stops competing with it (`apps/web/app/styles/layout.css`).
- **TikTok Sans with every axis, self-hosted**, the face the Goodpix reference uses; width marks rank. A local Arial face with TikTok Sans's measurements stands in while it loads, because next/font has no metrics for this family (`apps/web/app/layout.tsx`, `globals.css`).
- **The departure board, in the Goodpix language** (the owner's reference). Ink is the only fill and gold only draws, containers and photos are square and anything pressed is a pill, hairlines instead of boxes, and TikTok Sans's width marks rank while weight marks state. Each day reads as a departure board: condensed tabular times in a left column, the moves beside them. Motion explains a change once: the board flips in when a plan arrives, only the times an edit moved flip afterwards, and under reduced motion everything crossfades. The product record is `apps/web/PRODUCT.md` and the direction contract `apps/web/.impeccable/surfaces/app-page-tsx.md`.
- **Photos are real Wikimedia Commons photos, credited.** 73 places have a photo of that exact place, each checked by eye; the others show their city's photo inset on a mat and labelled, never as the place. Each credit names the author as they ask to be credited, links the licence and the Commons page. The photos are downloaded once at 500 and 960 px and served from the site (`data/place-photos.json`, `scripts/fetch-place-photos.ts`, `apps/web/lib/placePhotos.ts`); the service worker does not precache them. Two highlight photos (the Colosseum, the Duomo di Milano) carry Italy's cultural heritage restriction, which only binds commercial reproduction. The small photos (highlight tiles, a stop's thumbnail) carry no credit line: each opens a sheet with the photo large and its full credit, and "About this data" lists every credit. CC BY and BY-SA allow attribution "in any reasonable manner based on the medium", and a credit one tap away, named on the tile, is that (`apps/web/components/Highlights.tsx`).
- **Place summaries are written once by Claude and checked by code.** `pnpm summaries:generate` (`scripts/generate-place-summaries.ts`) sends Claude Opus 5.5 one place's own listing at a time (name, type, city, neighbourhood, description, tags, visit length, price level, rating, hours text) with structured output, at effort low since the model cannot turn thinking off, and saves each summary with the model id, the prompt version and the day on its own row, so writing a few places again (`--only=<ids>`) never relabels the rest (`apps/web/lib/placeSummaries.data.json`). A code check (`apps/web/lib/placeSummaryCheck.ts`) refuses a summary with a number, time, date, price, booking claim, meal, name or superlative the listing does not have (numbers only from the listing's own words, never its rating, price level or visit length; a word's forms count as one, so the listing's "Book" allows "booked"), "you" or "we", a dash, an exclamation mark, more than two sentences or over 220 characters, or a sentence that opens with anything but a plain opener or a word of the place's name, type or location, so neither a new name nor one of the listing's instructions ("Go early") can start a sentence. A refused summary is asked for once more with the reasons, then left out. A call that fails is no verdict on the place: it keeps its row and the run exits 1. The tests run the same check over the saved file. The first run (prompt v1, 2026-09-25) wrote all 103, 101 at the first try. Reading each against its listing found 11 that said more than the listing (its advice stated as fact, "It is run as a small-group class"; a quality moved onto something else, the museum's "quiet" onto its neighbourhood) or read badly; prompt v2 adds rules for those, and the 11 were written again with it, a few more than once until each read true to its listing. The check reads words, not meaning, so a person still reads what it passes. The sheets label it "Summary by AI, from the listing" and set it leaning, apart from the listing's own words. Alternatives: generating in the browser or the API per view (cost, latency and a model call per tap for text that never changes); no summary (the listing's own description is often a tip, not a description).

### Infrastructure

- **OIDC trust uses GitHub's immutable subject** with repository and owner ids, requires the owner's `actor_id`, and pins the deploy role to `deploy.yml` on `main` and the plan role to `ci.yml` on pull requests.
- **The plan role is a custom read list** with explicit denies; managed read-only policies would expose other stacks' data.
- **No HTTP API, WAF or CloudFront logs**: each needs an account-wide log policy in a shared account. The function logs every request that reaches it.
- **CSP**: `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'` because the static export's inline payloads change every build and headers cannot carry nonces; no `unsafe-eval`; `worker-src` and `manifest-src 'self'`.
- **`/api/*` is HTTPS only** (a redirected POST loses its body), and API errors are never cached at the edge.
- **The boundary works only for the function's own code** (`lambda:SourceFunctionArn`), so a changed trust policy gains nothing.
- **CI cannot delete** the distributions, the web bucket or the stack.
- **WAF**: plan calls 30 per IP per 5 minutes on any spelling of the path (day re-plans count toward the same limit: `/api/plan/day` starts with `/api/plan`, and a suffix match on `/plan/day` catches a path that starts with a back-reference), all calls 2000; the body-size rule blocks, the query-string size rule only counts so long share links load. Saving trips has no rule of its own yet, only the overall limit; a rule for `/api/trips` and `/trips` in `infra/terraform/platform/waf.tf` is a follow-up, left out of the round that added the gateway throttle. Like the plan limit, it must match any spelling of the path, encoded ones included: `POST /api/trip%73` reaches the handler.
- **Managed cache policies by literal id**, so tests catch an `/api/*` behavior that caches.
- **The API host reuses the site's headers policy**: one policy to review and pin.
- **Host names are limited to the `italy-planner.brdjx.com` subtree** in both roots.
- **Lambda**: `nodejs24.x` on arm64, 512 MB, 29 s timeout, 10 reserved instances; seven alarms, including handled server errors, the share of AI plans whose model call failed, and failed reads or writes of the trips table.
- **The trips table is retained, protected and item-private**: `DeletionPolicy` and `UpdateReplacePolicy` `Retain` and deletion protection on (a `CreateTable` and `UpdateTable` setting, so the deploy role needs nothing new; `check-infra-contract.py` fails CI without any of the three, since the deploy role cannot delete tables), encryption with DynamoDB's AWS owned key (a KMS key would need rights CI is denied), the function limited to `GetItem` and `PutItem`, and CI to managing tables named `italy-planner-*` without item rights. The bootstrap change is applied by an admin before the deploy that creates the table.
- **SAM uses its own artifacts bucket** and no `sam build`, so the deployed bundle is the tested bundle.
- **Every route throttle names a route that exists**: `check-infra-contract.py` fails CI when a `RouteSettings` key under the HTTP API has no matching event, since that fails the SAM deploy part way.

### CI/CD

- **Every third-party action is pinned to a commit SHA**; Dependabot proposes updates weekly after a 7-day cooldown.
- **`ci-ok` rolls every CI job into one status**: it fails on a cancelled run and lets only the plan job skip. A deploy starts only after a successful `ci` run for a push to `main` (the `gate` job in `deploy.yml`). Making `ci-ok` a required check needs branch protection, which the repository's GitHub plan does not offer yet ([deploy.md](deploy.md#github-plan)); until then, merge only green pull requests.
- **gitleaks, trivy, actionlint, Terraform and SAM CLI are downloaded and checked against pinned SHA-256 digests**, never restored from a cache.
- **Only commits on `main` deploy**, rollbacks included, and an automatic deploy only moves production forward (`deploy-guard.sh`).
- **Build and deploy are separate jobs**: third-party build code runs without credentials; the deploy job runs pinned tools on the artifacts.
- **Deploy scripts come from the workflow's own commit**, so a rollback runs today's deploy logic.
- **`samconfig.toml` is the single source of deploy parameters**; CI appends `GitSha` last.
- **Pull requests cancel superseded runs; every push to `main` gets its own run.**
- **Accepted scanner findings are suppressed inline** with a reason, never in a central ignore file.
- **Live evals never run on pull requests or on a schedule** and never gate a merge or a deploy; the eval workflow is started by hand, so nothing spends on the key unattended; CI runs the offline replay, which enforces plan validity only; model quality numbers are reported, not enforced.

### Testing

- **Planner properties run 500 times with a fixed seed** in every run and 5,000 with `pnpm test:props`; a failure prints its seed.
- **Plans are memoized per test file**, so each property keeps its own name at a third of the CPU.
- **Performance is asserted at 5 times the budget**, which catches a blow-up without flaking.
- **E2E blocks CI again** (T62, 2026-09-25). During the redesign it reported without blocking (owner's call, 2026-09-24), from a workflow of its own after a timed-out run held back a deploy. With the specs rewritten for the one-column page it is a job in `ci.yml` again, `ci-ok` waits for it, and its 30 minute limit is well over a green run on the runner (14 to 18 minutes).
- **E2E runs under the production CSP and routing** through `e2e/serve.mjs`, with a fixed clock, one rate-limit bucket per test, and specs routed to projects by file name instead of being skipped.
- **Service workers are blocked on device projects** and tested in their own project.
- **Any console error fails an E2E test**, except the browser's own line for an injected failed load.
- **Coverage floors per area**, so one well-covered package cannot hide another.
